STAY SAFE FROM GODZILLA MELBOURNE!

There is a super el niño, dubbed the "Godzilla El Niño" that will be very dangerous hitting a lot of the southern hemisphere soon, so this website is to compile info for my local city and most of the state Melbourne Victoria.

Are you prepared?

- Do you have working smoke detectors installed?
- How recently did you check the battery?

- Do you have a safe location to go to?
- What can you bring?
- How will you get there?

- How will you know when to act?
- Install VicEmergency App.
- Listen to local Radio.
- Stay in touch with family, friends and neighbours.
- Be aware of Fire Danger Rating in your area.

- Do you have a plan?
- Who is aware of your plan?
- Coordinate your plan with those in your community I.E family, neighbours and friends.
- Expect the unexpected: Wind Change, Road Closure, etc.

My Fire Plan

In preparation for a fire I will:
1. Stay aware of Fire Danger Rating and local weather via VicEmergency and Radio.
2. Keep tabs on people around me. Make sure they are safe as well.
3. Make sure that no electrical devices are overheating or left on.
4. Make sure the smoke alarms in my house have working batteries.
5. Clear plant/flammable debris from around my house.
6. Make sure (if any) fire safety equipment is in an accessible location and works. (Fire blanket / Fire extinguisher).
7. Pre-pack car with basic first aid, water and supplies in case of emergency.
8. Find or plan meeting point/ safe zone from fire with family, community and friends.


In the event of a fire I will:
1. Be alerted by verified smoke alarms (if house fire) or VicEmergency (if wildfire).
2. Remember the plan I have made.
3. Alert immediate family in the house.
4. Dial 000 to alert Emergency Services.
5. Identify fire source (if house fire).
6. If able (very small local fire ONLY) extinguish/control fire using fire blanket and/or fire extinguisher.
7. If unable to contain house fire, or in event of wildfire, gather family and pets outside of the house.
8. Alert neighbours and local friends to spread awareness and see if they are safe or need help.
9. (If possible) escape area via car (with basic supplies) to predetermined safe zone.

My Flood Plan

In preparation for a flood I will:
1. Stay aware of flood warnings issued by news/radio and VicEmergency.
2. Inform changes with friends, neighbours and family.
3. Prepare each door or potential entrance for flood water with sandbags or blocks.
4. Store fresh drinking water in the event of sudden drought or plumbing issues.
5. Avoid having anything electrical touching the ground.
6. Pre-pack car with basic first aid, water and supplies in case of emergency.
7. Plan evacuation location to somewhere high above water level.


In the event of a flood I will:
1. Stay aware of the water level outside.
2. Dial 000 to alert Emergency Services.
3. Keep tabs on people around me. Make sure they are safe as well.
4. Secure doors to prevent water coming in (stuffing towels or clothes under doors).
5. Always wear shoes and long pants when dealing with flood water.
6. Be cautious with the unknown depth and contents of flood water when walking or driving. Don't know? Don't risk it.
7. If flooding persists or worsens beyond my control I will evacuate my home with my family and pets and make my way to pre-determined higher ground.

My Drought Plan

In preparation for a drought I will:
1. Stay aware of drought warnings issued by news/radio and VicEmergency.
2. Stock up on packaged fresh drinking water in the event of rationed/strained water supply.
3. Discuss water usage with family to prepare for water strain. (Shorter showers, manual washing if possible).
4. Clear plant debris from around house, dry leaves are flammable.
5. Make sure I have fans/ things to keep cool during hot dry weather.
6. Check if friends and neighbors are prepared.
7. Stay alert for either floods or fires that are very likely to follow.


In the event of a drought I will:
1. Be aware in days leading up to it due to radio/news and VicEmergency.
2. Immediately discuss water limiting with family. (Shorter showers, manual washing if possible).
3. Ration stockpiled water according to necessity.
4. Try to stay cool temperature to reduce sweating/dehydration and shower less if needed.
5. Make sure surrounding dry plant matter and flammables are away from the house to prevent fire.
6. Make sure pets aren't dehydrated or overheating.
7. Check in with friends and neighbors to see if they are prepared or need help.
8. Stay alert for either floods or fires that are very likely to follow.

For live incidents and emergency warnings in Victoria, visit VicEmergency or call the VicEmergency Hotline on 1800 226 226. In an emergency, always call 000. https://emergency.vic.gov.au/respond/

Community Alert Sirens are used as part of Victoria’s warning system for all hazards – including flood, fire and storm. They are only used when there is imminent threat to the community.

Short Siren (up to 90 seconds) – indicates a CFA Brigade has responded to an emergency incident nearby. Stay informed.

Extended Siren (5 minutes) – indicates that a current emergency has been identified in the local area and people should seek further information immediately. The emergency incident will impact people in this area.

Sirens are not stand alone means of warning the community and do not replace the need for people to monitor the conditions around them, particularly on hot, dry, windy days.
A number of factors will limit who will hear the siren including adverse weather conditions, so you may not hear the siren.
